The Business Analyst

The translator between the business and the software. The BA turns vague wants, tribal knowledge, and conflicting departmental habits into a clear, prioritized, testable definition of what the Odoo system must do — the specification everything downstream is built and tested against. Their product is not a document; it is shared understanding.

Module 1

What the role really is

The Business Analyst sits in the gap between two languages. On one side is the business — a warehouse supervisor who says "we always reserve stock for the big accounts first," a finance lead who says "we can't close the month until the consignment stock is reconciled." On the other side is Odoo — a system of models, fields, workflows, and configuration switches that knows nothing about "big accounts" until someone defines what that means precisely. The BA is the person who makes those two sides mean the same thing.

Concretely, the BA produces the requirements that everything downstream is built against. The Functional Consultant configures Odoo to satisfy them. The developer codes against the functional specs derived from them. The UAT testers prove the system meets them. The PM tracks scope against them. If the requirements are wrong or fuzzy, every one of those activities inherits the error — and the cost of fixing a misunderstanding multiplies at each stage.

The BA's deliverable is not the Business Requirements Document. It is the moment when the client, the consultant, and the developer all picture the same system. The document is just the evidence that the shared understanding exists.

On an Odoo project the BA connects three things that naturally drift apart: what people say they want, what the business actually needs to operate, and what Odoo can do as standard. Holding all three in view at once is the essence of the role.

Definition

Business Analysis is the practice of enabling change in an organization by defining needs and recommending solutions that deliver value to stakeholders. In an ERP context it means understanding how the business runs today, defining how it should run on the new system, and specifying that target precisely enough that it can be configured and verified. The BA is the discipline of turning intent into requirements.

Module 2

The BA mindset

Tools and templates can be learned quickly. What separates a strong BA is a set of operating instincts about how to listen, question, and reason.

Relentless curiosity

The BA assumes they don't yet understand and keeps digging. "Walk me through what happens next." "Show me the actual spreadsheet." Understanding lives in the details the client forgot to mention because they're obvious to them.

Ask "why", not just "what"

A client asks for a custom approval screen. The BA asks why — and learns the real need is an audit trail, which Odoo's chatter and activity log already provide. The stated request is rarely the real requirement.

Separate wants from needs

Stakeholders describe solutions ("I want a button here") when they mean problems ("I keep forgetting to follow up"). The BA's job is to peel the request back to the underlying business need before solutioning.

Neutrality between departments

Sales and Finance will disagree about how a credit limit should work. The BA is not an advocate for either; they surface the conflict, frame the trade-off, and get the right person to decide. Taking sides corrupts the requirements.

Precision in language

"The order should be approved quickly" is not a requirement. "Orders over €10,000 require manager approval within 4 business hours" is. The BA hunts down every vague word — quickly, easily, sometimes, etc. — and pins it to something testable.

Comfort with ambiguity, intolerance of leaving it

Early discovery is messy and contradictory. The BA can sit in that fog without panicking — but never ships it. They drive every open question to a documented, agreed answer.

Module 3

Requirements engineering — the discipline

Requirements engineering is the structured practice at the heart of the BA role. It is not one activity but five, performed continuously rather than in a strict line.

ActivityWhat it producesOn a TechNext Odoo project
1. ElicitationRaw needs drawn out of stakeholders and documents.Discovery workshops with the sales, warehouse, and finance teams; reading their existing process docs and sample invoices.
2. AnalysisNeeds structured, conflicts resolved, gaps found.Reconciling that Sales wants instant order confirmation while Finance wants a credit check first — and modelling how both fit in the Odoo Sales flow.
3. SpecificationRequirements written down clearly and unambiguously.The BRD and functional requirements, user stories, acceptance criteria — the artifacts the FC configures against.
4. ValidationConfirmation the requirements are correct and complete.Walking the written requirements back to the client for sign-off; confirming nothing was misheard before build starts.
5. ManagementRequirements tracked, versioned, and traced as they change.The Requirements Traceability Matrix linking each requirement to its config and its UAT test; routing changes through the PM's change control.

Functional vs. non-functional requirements

A functional requirement describes what the system must do: "The system shall generate a delivery order automatically when a sales order is confirmed." A non-functional requirement describes how well it must do it — performance, security, usability, availability, compliance: "Invoice PDFs must render within 3 seconds," or "Only the Finance group may post journal entries." On ERP projects non-functional requirements are routinely forgotten until they bite — the BA is the one who asks about volumes, concurrent users, audit, and access control up front.

The three levels of requirement

  • Business requirements — the high-level goals of the organization. "Cut order-to-cash cycle time from 6 days to 2." The why.
  • Stakeholder requirements — what a particular group needs to meet those goals. "The sales rep needs to see live stock availability when quoting." The who and what they need.
  • Solution requirements — the specific behaviour the system must deliver, split into functional and non-functional. "The quotation line shall display Forecasted Quantity from the product's stock." The concrete what.
ℹ️ Why levels matter

Tracing a solution requirement back up to a business goal is the BA's defense against gold-plating. If a requested feature can't be linked to a real business need, it's a candidate for the "do we actually need this?" conversation — and on a fit-to-standard Odoo project, that conversation saves customizations.

Module 4

Elicitation techniques

Elicitation is the craft of drawing out requirements that stakeholders can't simply hand over — because they've never written them down, disagree about them, or don't consciously know them. No single technique is enough; the BA combines several.

TechniqueWhat it isWhen to use it
InterviewsOne-on-one structured or open conversation with a stakeholder.To go deep with a key user or sponsor, surface sensitive issues, and understand individual pain points without group pressure.
WorkshopsFacilitated group session with several stakeholders together.To map a cross-department process end-to-end, surface disagreements live, and build shared agreement quickly. The workhorse of Odoo discovery.
Observation / job-shadowingWatching people actually do the work at their desk.When stated process and real process differ — to catch the workarounds and "we don't really do it that way" steps people forget to mention.
Document analysisStudying existing forms, reports, spreadsheets, procedures, and the legacy system.To extract current data structures, business rules buried in spreadsheets, and the real field set needed in Odoo (e.g. what's actually on their invoice template).
Surveys / questionnairesStructured written questions sent to many respondents.When many users span sites or shifts and you need breadth — e.g. capturing each branch's local quirks before workshops.
PrototypingShowing a draft of the working system and gathering reactions.When users can't describe what they want in the abstract but know it when they see it — configure standard Odoo and let them react.
💡 Pro tip

The most valuable technique on an Odoo project is often prototyping with standard Odoo. Rather than spend weeks documenting an abstract requirement, the BA and FC stand up the standard Sales or Inventory flow and put it in front of users. Their reactions ("yes but we also need…") elicit better requirements in an hour than a survey does in a week — and it naturally pulls the conversation toward fit-to-standard.

Module 5

Process modelling: as-is and to-be

Requirements don't live in isolation — they live inside business processes. The BA models those processes to understand how work actually flows, where it breaks, and how it should flow on Odoo.

The vocabulary

  • BPMN (Business Process Model and Notation) — a standard visual language for processes: rounded rectangles for activities, diamonds for decisions/gateways, circles for start/end events, arrows for flow.
  • Swimlanes — horizontal or vertical bands showing who performs each step (Sales, Warehouse, Finance, the system). They make handoffs and hand-backs visible — and handoffs are where processes leak.
  • As-is — the current-state map: how the business operates today, warts and all.
  • To-be — the future-state map: how the process will run on Odoo after go-live.
  • Value-stream thinking — looking at the end-to-end flow and asking which steps add value and which are waste (re-keying, waiting, checking work twice) to be designed out, not carried over.

Worked TechNext example: quote-to-cash

A TechNext client, a mid-size industrial distributor, runs quote-to-cash on spreadsheets and a legacy accounting package. Here is the BA's analysis.

⚠️ As-is — current state pain points

A rep builds a quote in Excel and emails it. To check stock they phone the warehouse. On acceptance, the order is re-typed into the accounting system and again into a warehouse spreadsheet. Finance manually checks each customer's balance against a separate aging report before release. Invoices are created by copy-pasting the order into a Word template. The same order data is re-keyed four times, stock figures are stale by the time the customer accepts, and credit checks are inconsistent because they depend on which clerk did them.

⚙️ To-be in Odoo — the TechNext angle

In the to-be model, the rep builds the quotation in Odoo Sales, where the line items show live Forecasted Quantity from Inventory — no phone call. A configured credit-limit rule on the customer (res.partner) blocks confirmation when the balance is exceeded, applied consistently by the system, not a clerk. Confirming the order auto-generates the delivery order in Inventory and lets Finance create the invoice in Accounting directly from the order with one click. The data is entered once and flows through. The four re-keys collapse to zero; the BA's to-be map is essentially a description of standard Odoo, which is exactly the fit-to-standard outcome to aim for.

Notice the discipline: the BA does not draw the to-be as "the old process but on a computer." They redesign around what Odoo does natively, eliminating the waste the as-is map exposed. The to-be map then becomes the backbone of the requirements and the UAT scenarios.

Module 6

Fit-gap analysis

Fit-gap is where requirements meet reality. Every requirement is held up against standard Odoo and classified. This is the single most commercially important analysis on the project, because it decides what gets configured (cheap, fast, upgrade-safe) versus what gets built (expensive, slow, a maintenance liability).

The three classifications

  • Fit — standard Odoo already does this with configuration only. No development. The goal is to maximize this column.
  • Gap (config / Studio) — not available out of the box, but achievable with Odoo Studio, automated actions, server actions, or configuration without custom code.
  • Custom — genuinely needs developer-built code: a custom module, a non-trivial integration, or complex logic Studio can't express.
RequirementClassificationHow it's met
Confirming a sales order should reserve and ship stock.FitStandard Sales → Inventory delivery flow. Configuration only.
Block order confirmation when a customer exceeds their credit limit.FitStandard credit-limit setting on res.partner in Accounting.
Add a "Site Survey Date" field on the sales order and show it on the PDF.GapAdd the field and edit the quotation report in Odoo Studio — no code.
Auto-create a follow-up Project task when an order over €50k is confirmed.GapAn automated action / server action triggered on order confirmation — configuration.
Real-time two-way sync with the client's bespoke logistics platform.CustomA developer-built integration module against the external API.
A proprietary, multi-tier rebate calculation unique to this client.CustomCustom code; no standard or Studio equivalent.

The fit-to-standard principle

The governing philosophy is fit-to-standard: prefer changing the process to fit Odoo over changing Odoo to fit the process. Every gap and every custom item is a future cost — it must be re-tested at each Odoo version upgrade and it adds risk. So the BA's instinct on hearing a gap is to challenge it before accepting it: Is this requirement real, or just "the way we've always done it"? Would a standard Odoo feature, slightly differently used, satisfy the underlying need? Is the value of the customization worth its lifetime cost?

⚙️ The TechNext angle

Because TechNext starts from pre-built vertical templates, a large share of requirements land in the Fit column before fit-gap even begins — "standard Odoo" is already 80% of the answer. The BA's job becomes triaging the remaining 20%: pushing hard to convert gaps into fits through process change, reserving genuine custom development for the handful of items that truly differentiate the client's business. Every gap defended into a fit protects the project's margin and the client's upgrade path.

Module 7

The BA across the eight phases

Here is exactly what the BA does in each phase of the Odoo lifecycle. The BA is heaviest in phase 2 (discovery) and stays deeply involved through data mapping in phase 5 and test-case authoring in phase 6.

1

Initiation & kickoff

A consulting role here. The BA helps shape the high-level scope and success criteria in the charter, identifies which stakeholders and business areas must be involved in discovery, and starts the glossary so everyone uses the client's terms consistently from day one. They prepare the discovery plan — which workshops, who attends, what to read first.

2

Discovery & analysis

The BA's heaviest phase — they lead it. Run the elicitation: workshops, interviews, observation, document analysis. Map the as-is processes, define the to-be on Odoo, and document business and functional requirements. Run the fit-gap analysis, classifying every requirement as fit/gap/custom. Prioritize with MoSCoW. Produce and validate the BRD. This is where the project's success is largely decided.

3

Solution design

Consulting role; the FC leads. The BA stays close to ensure the design genuinely satisfies the requirements and to answer the FC's "what did the client mean here?" questions. They clarify edge cases, confirm business rules, and update requirements when design reveals a gap. They keep the requirements and the emerging design traceable to each other.

4

Build & configuration

Mostly informed, but actively available. As the FC configures and developers build, the BA fields clarification questions, validates that what's being built matches the intent, and keeps the requirements traceability matrix current as approved changes land. They prepare the test cases that phase 6 will run.

5

Data migration

A second heavy phase for the BA — they own the data mapping. Define the source-to-target field mapping from the legacy system to Odoo models (res.partner, product.template, account.move…), specify transformation and deduplication rules, run the data quality assessment, and validate migrated data against source. The FC executes the load; the BA designs and verifies the map.

6

Testing & UAT

The BA writes the UAT test cases — derived directly from the requirements and the to-be process maps, so testing proves the system does what was agreed. They support client testers, help triage whether a reported defect is a real requirement miss or a misunderstanding, and confirm each requirement is covered by at least one passing test via the traceability matrix.

7

Training & go-live

Consulting role. Because the BA understands the to-be processes better than anyone, they often help build training material and user guides that follow the real workflows. They support final data validation at cutover and remain on hand to answer "is this behaving as specified?" questions during the switch.

8

Hypercare & support

Largely informed. The BA helps interpret early-life issues: is this a defect, a training gap, or a missed requirement? They capture continuous-improvement requests for the next iteration and feed lessons back into the glossary and requirement templates.

Module 8

Deliverables & artifacts

These are the tangible documents the BA produces and maintains. Each captures and protects a piece of the shared understanding.

Business Requirements Document (BRD)

The high-level statement of business goals, scope, and what success looks like in business terms. The reference everyone returns to for "why are we doing this?"

Functional Requirements Specification

The detailed list of what the system must do, requirement by requirement, with priorities — the FC's primary input for configuration.

As-is / to-be process maps

BPMN swimlane diagrams of the current and future state for each in-scope process — the backbone of design and UAT.

Gap-fit analysis

Every requirement classified fit / gap / custom, with the chosen approach and rationale — the basis for scope and effort.

User stories & use cases

Requirements expressed from the user's point of view with acceptance criteria, plus step-by-step use cases for complex flows.

Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)

The grid linking each requirement to its design, its configuration, and its UAT test — proof that nothing was dropped and everything is tested.

Data mapping specification

Source-to-target mapping of legacy fields to Odoo fields with transformation and dedup rules — the blueprint for migration.

UAT test cases

Scenario-based scripts, traceable to requirements, that the client runs to formally accept the system.

Glossary / data dictionary

Agreed definitions of business terms and the meaning, type, and source of each data field — kills "we each meant something different by 'customer'."

Module 9

Writing great requirements

A requirement is only useful if it can be built and tested without re-interpretation. The craft of writing them is what separates a BA from a scribe.

User stories & acceptance criteria

The user-story format keeps requirements grounded in real value: "As a sales rep, I want to see live stock availability on the quotation line, so that I don't promise stock we don't have." The story states who, what, and why. It is paired with acceptance criteria — the concrete, testable conditions that prove it's done: "Given a product with 5 units forecasted, when I add it to a quotation, then the line shows Forecasted Quantity = 5; and when forecasted quantity is zero, the line shows a stock warning."

Quality checklists — INVEST and SMART

Two mnemonics keep requirements honest. A good user story is INVEST: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable. A good requirement statement is SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Both converge on the same demand — a requirement you can't test is not finished.

  • Independent — can be understood and built without depending on another story's wording.
  • Negotiable — captures the need, not a locked-in implementation; leaves room for the fit-to-standard conversation.
  • Valuable — traces up to a real business goal.
  • Estimable — clear enough that the FC can size the effort.
  • Small — fits in a delivery cycle; large items are split.
  • Testable — has acceptance criteria a tester can pass or fail unambiguously.
⚠️ Ambiguity is the most expensive defect

A misread requirement isn't caught until UAT — after it's been designed, configured, possibly coded, and data-mapped around. By then the fix touches every one of those layers. The word "etc." in a requirement, an undefined "approval", a "fast" with no number — each is a landmine. The cheapest moment to fix a requirement is before anyone builds against it. The BA hunts ambiguity like a defect, because that is exactly what it is.

Prioritization with MoSCoW

Not every requirement is equal, and fixed-scope packages force choices. MoSCoW sorts them: Must have (the system fails without it), Should have (important but a workaround exists), Could have (nice, dropped first under pressure), Won't have this time (explicitly out of scope, parked for a later phase). Agreeing MoSCoW with the sponsor early gives the PM a principled way to protect the timeline when something has to give — you cut from the bottom, by agreement, not by accident.

Module 10

Data analysis & migration mapping

Data migration is where Odoo projects most often slip, and the BA is central to it. The system can be perfectly configured, but if the customers, products, and open balances loaded into it are wrong, the business won't trust it — and trust lost at go-live is hard to win back.

Source-to-target mapping

The core artifact is the source-to-target mapping specification: for every field in the legacy data, where it lands in Odoo, and how it's transformed on the way. A simplified extract:

Legacy fieldOdoo model.fieldTransformation rule
CUST_NAMEres.partner.nameTrim whitespace; title-case; flag blanks for review.
CUST_TYPE (C/V/B)res.partner.customer_rank / supplier_rankMap C→customer, V→vendor, B→both; default 0 otherwise.
ITEM_CODEproduct.template.default_codeUppercase; must be unique — duplicates flagged.
OPEN_INV_BALaccount.move (open entries)Load as open customer invoices at migration date; reconcile total to legacy aging report.

Data quality assessment & deduplication

Before mapping, the BA profiles the legacy data: how many records are missing required fields, how many customers are near-duplicates ("ACME Ltd", "Acme Limited", "ACME LTD."), how stale the records are. They define deduplication rules (match on tax ID first, then normalized name + postcode), set cleansing rules, and decide what history actually needs to migrate versus what can be archived — migrating ten years of dead records is pure cost and risk. Crucially, the BA makes data quality the client's owned responsibility, surfaced early, because only the client can decide which "ACME" is the real one.

💡 Pro tip

Always run at least one trial migration into a staging environment well before go-live, then reconcile loaded totals back to a legacy report (e.g. total open receivables must match the aging report to the cent). A trial that surfaces a mapping error in week three is a fix; the same error discovered at cutover is a postponed go-live.

⚙️ The TechNext angle

TechNext records discovery sessions and uses AI to draft first-pass requirements from the transcripts — turning a two-hour workshop into a structured set of candidate user stories in minutes. But the draft is exactly that: a draft. A human BA validates every AI-drafted requirement against the client before it enters the BRD, because the costliest error on the project is a confidently-worded requirement that the client never actually meant. Automate the typing; never automate the understanding.

Module 12

Skills that matter

The BA role is a blend of analytical technique and human listening. The hard skills get you in the room; the soft skills get you the truth.

Hard skills

Requirements elicitation Process modelling / BPMN Data analysis Odoo functional knowledge Documentation SQL / spreadsheet literacy Fit-gap analysis

Soft skills (the decisive ones)

Active listening Questioning Communication Facilitation Stakeholder empathy Attention to detail Neutrality

A BA does not need to configure Odoo to expert level — but they must know it well enough to recognize when a requirement is a fit, to challenge an unnecessary gap, and to speak the FC's language. Enough Odoo functional literacy to steer requirements toward standard is the bar, paired with the listening skill to be sure they've understood what the business truly needs.

Module 14

Boundaries & the TechNext lean model

Knowing where the BA stops is as important as knowing what they do. The BA owns the what; the PM owns the when and cost; the FC owns the how.

  • BA vs. PM: the BA defines what the business needs and how to phrase it testably; the PM owns when it's delivered and at what cost. The BA doesn't manage the budget or the schedule; the PM doesn't write requirements — but the PM routes every requirement change through change control.
  • BA vs. FC: the BA defines the need in business terms ("orders over a limit must be approved"); the FC decides how Odoo solves it ("a configured approval rule on the sales order"). The handoff between them — clear, prioritized, testable requirements — is one of the project's most critical (see the handoffs). The two roles overlap heavily, and in many shops they merge.

Metrics & KPIs the BA tracks

The BA's quality is measurable. These indicators reveal whether the requirements work is actually protecting the project.

Churn
Requirements stability — how much do requirements change after sign-off?
% Traced
Requirements traced to a UAT test in the RTM
Fit %
Fit-to-standard ratio — requirements met by standard Odoo
Gap defects
Defects traced back to a requirement gap or ambiguity
UAT pass
First-run UAT pass rate against the test cases
Sign-off
Cycle time to validate and sign off requirements

The most telling leading indicator is requirements churn after sign-off: heavy late changes signal discovery didn't truly land, and every one of them ripples through design, build, data, and tests. Low churn with high traceability is the signature of BA work that's doing its job.

⚙️ How the role is absorbed in TechNext's A-Team

In TechNext's lean, automation-first model there is rarely a standalone BA seat. The BA and FC roles merge into a single Solutions Engineer who both defines the need and decides how Odoo meets it — the overlap between the two roles is large enough that splitting them adds handoffs, not value. Automation carries the toil: discovery transcripts become draft requirements, vertical templates pre-classify most requirements as fits, and test cases generate from the to-be maps. What stays irreducibly human is the judgment in this course — drawing out the real need, the neutral read across departments, the fit-to-standard challenge, and the validation that a written requirement truly means what the client meant. Automate the scope drafting; never automate the understanding.
