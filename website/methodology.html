Module 0.1

Methodologies — and how TechNext chooses

An ERP implementation is not just software installation; it is organizational change delivered through software. Three broad methodologies are used in the industry, and TechNext blends them.

Waterfall

Sequential phases, heavy upfront documentation, fixed scope. Predictable and easy to price, but unforgiving of discovery-stage mistakes. Good for small, well-understood scopes.

Agile / iterative

Configure in sprints, demo early and often, let requirements firm up through feedback. Great for complex or fuzzy scopes; harder to fix-price.

Odoo's own approach

Odoo SA promotes a fast, iterative method: configure standard Odoo first, show it to users early, and only customize what truly can't be covered by standard features ("fit-to-standard").

⚙️ The TechNext method

TechNext runs a hybrid, fit-to-standard, iterative model: fixed-scope productized packages (Waterfall-like commercial certainty) delivered through short configure-demo-refine loops (Agile execution), starting from pre-built vertical templates so "standard Odoo" is already 80% of the answer. Customize last, not first.

Module 0.2

The eight phases of an Odoo project

This is the spine of the whole curriculum. Each role course revisits these exact phases from its own point of view.

1

Initiation & kickoff

Charter, sponsor alignment, success criteria, scope baseline, team and governance set up. The project gets its definition of "done" and "won."

2

Discovery & analysis

Elicit and document the business processes (as-is), define the target (to-be), run fit-gap against Odoo, agree on what's standard vs. custom.

3

Solution design

Decide how Odoo will deliver each requirement: module selection, configuration approach, integrations, data model, and functional specs for any custom development.

4

Build & configuration

Configure Odoo, develop the approved customizations and integrations, and assemble the solution in a staging environment.

5

Data migration

Extract, clean, map, transform, and load legacy data (customers, products, open invoices, stock, history) — and reconcile it.

6

Testing & UAT

Functional testing, integration testing, and User Acceptance Testing where the client signs off that the system does what was agreed.

7

Training & go-live

Train end users and key users, finalize the cutover plan, migrate production data, flip the switch, and start operating on Odoo.

8

Hypercare & support

Intensive post-go-live support, rapid issue resolution, stabilization, then transition to steady-state support and continuous improvement.

Module 0.4

The critical handoffs (where projects die)

Most failed ERP projects fail at a handoff, not in the work itself. The three big ones:

  • BA → FC: requirements that are clear, prioritized, and testable. A vague requirement becomes a wrong configuration becomes a failed UAT.
  • FC / SA → Developer: a functional spec (what it should do) plus a technical design (how it's built) precise enough that a developer builds the right thing once, as a clean, upgrade-safe extension. Ambiguity here is the most expensive ambiguity on the project.
  • Project → Client (go-live): a system the users were trained on, data they trust, and a support net for week one. Skipping training or hypercare is how a technically-successful project still "fails."
Module 0.5

Where automation fits (the TechNext layer)

TechNext's lean model wraps this lifecycle in automation so a small team can run it. As you read each role course, notice which tasks are judgment (kept human) and which are toil (automated):

Automated / templated

  • Demo environment provisioning per industry
  • Discovery transcript → draft requirements
  • Vertical configuration loaded as config-as-code
  • Test case & UAT script generation
  • Status reporting to a client portal
  • Tier-1 support answers

Kept human (judgment)

  • The client relationship and trust
  • Solution design for the novel 20%
  • Validating AI-drafted requirements & specs
  • Change management and training empathy
  • Sign-off on anything client-facing
  • Risk and commercial decisions
